# Live Trigger Safety Checks

Use this file before recommending a TM-6 PRO setup as Sunday-ready.

## Trigger Behavior

Check:

- Soft, normal, and hard hits all trigger musically.
- Fast doubles and fills do not mistrigger or sound mechanical.
- Kick does not double-trigger from head rebound.
- Snare does not false-trigger from kick or toms.
- Floor tom does not false-trigger from kick, sub, or stage vibration.
- Threshold is high enough to reject bleed but low enough for intentional soft hits.
- Sensitivity preserves useful dynamics.
- Crosstalk cancellation is adjusted only as much as needed.

## Musical Fit

Check:

- The acoustic drum still feels like the main source unless the user wants a sample-forward sound.
- The sample supports the active aimpoint.
- Kick and floor tom do not mask bass guitar or low keys.
- Snare crack does not steal attention from the lead vocal.
- Long tails do not clutter pads, reverbs, prayer moments, or transitions.
- Muting the trigger layer makes the mix smaller, not broken.

## Room And Stream Split

Check:

- FOH and livestream need the same layer level only if the room and stream hear similar acoustic drum energy.
- If the room already has plenty of acoustic drum, keep trigger layers quieter in FOH and consider more in stream.
- If routing allows, use direct outputs or separate stream processing for sample support.
- If routing is shared, choose conservative sounds that work in both paths.

## Gain And Processing

Check:

- No trigger channel clips on hard hits.
- Pad compressor is not flattening dynamics beyond the aimpoint.
- Transient attack does not make the drum harsh.
- Decay/release does not create low-frequency buildup.
- Master comp/limiter movement is not driven by trigger layers.
- Effects are intentional and section-appropriate.

## Rehearsal Test Procedure

1. Start with acoustic mics only and note the missing quality.
2. Add the trigger layer at a very low level.
3. Raise until the missing quality is solved.
4. Lower slightly and recheck in the full band.
5. Test the loudest chorus and the sparsest verse.
6. Mute the layer for four bars, then unmute it. If the layer calls attention to itself, reduce blend, shorten decay, or choose a subtler sound.

## Approval Language

Use one of these:

- "Rehearsal-safe": usable for rehearsal or virtual soundcheck, but not proven in service.
- "Sunday-safe": checked in context with band, vocals, and deployment path.
- "Stream-safe only": useful in the broadcast mix but too much or unnecessary in the room.
- "Special-use only": good for a song section or effect, not a default kit layer.

## Common Fixes

- Too fake: lower blend, choose a more natural family, reduce transient, tune closer to the acoustic drum.
- Too boomy: shorten decay, reduce low shelf/sub, choose a tighter sample.
- Too clicky: reduce transient, choose warmer beater, lower high-mid EQ.
- Too inconsistent: adjust sensitivity/curve and compression before raising sample level.
- Too mechanical: preserve dynamics, avoid loud samples on ghost notes, choose a less processed layer.
- Too disconnected: tune the sample, reduce latency-like perception by shortening attack/tail, verify trigger settings.
