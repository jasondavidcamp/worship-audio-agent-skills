# Livestream Translation Playbook

Use this when a room mix must translate online, or when diagnosing a stream that is thin, dead, washy, harsh, inconsistent, speech-heavy, or disconnected from the congregation.

## First Principle

The FOH mix reinforces what already exists in the room. The stream mix must recreate the whole experience for someone who has no room, no PA, no congregation around them, and often small speakers.

## Choose The Workflow

House mix to stream:

- Use only when staffing and equipment are minimal.
- Expect balance problems because acoustic sources may be loud in the room but weak online.
- Add output compression carefully for speech/music level swings, but do not expect it to fix missing instruments.
- Consider it a baseline or fallback, not a finished broadcast strategy.

Aux or matrix stream mix from FOH:

- Good middle ground for many churches.
- Use post-fader sends when FOH moves should help the stream follow the service.
- Offset the stream balance for sources that are underrepresented in FOH: drums, guitars, keys, congregational mics, or quiet instruments.
- Keep stream-only sources out of the room mix when they would cause feedback or wash.

Dedicated broadcast mix:

- Best control when the church has staffing, monitoring, and communication.
- Requires an isolated listening position, reliable communication with FOH/stage, and clear service cues.
- Best for active musical decisions, not just better gear.

Remote or delayed mix:

- Favor group-level moves over channel-by-channel riding when latency makes detailed timing unreliable.
- Build virtual groups or DCAs for broadcast drums, vocals, guitars/keys, tracks, and congregation.
- Use individual channel access only for clear problems that survive the delay.

## Stream Balance Priorities

1. Spoken word and lead vocal remain intelligible.
2. Music has a complete foundation: drums, bass, harmonic bed, lead/BGV relationship.
3. Congregational/audience mics provide place and participation.
4. Dynamics are controlled enough that viewers do not chase volume.
5. Harshness, cymbal wash, and low-end overload do not trigger fatigue or platform compression problems.

## Audience And Room Mics

Audience mics are often the difference between a direct board feed and an online worship experience.

Use them to add:

- Congregational singing.
- Applause, prayer response, and room life.
- Size and emotional context in quiet moments.

Control them carefully:

- Keep them out of FOH unless intentionally needed.
- Pull them down during dense band sections if they smear vocals.
- Bring them up during quiet breaks, prayer, applause, and congregation-led moments.
- Mute or reduce them during speech when they add echo, noise, or distraction.
- Check phase, timing, stereo image, and mono compatibility before trusting them.

## Common Stream Diagnoses

Stream feels dead:

- Add audience/room mics or short ambience after vocal clarity is stable.
- Check whether the stream is only a direct board mix with no room context.

Stream is washy:

- Pull down audience mics during dense music.
- Mute or reduce room mics during speech.
- Filter room mics so low-mid room buildup does not cover vocals.

Vocals disappear online but are fine in the room:

- The room may be carrying vocal or band information that the stream feed does not.
- Rebalance stream sends rather than changing FOH tone.
- Check whether acoustic drums/guitars are missing from the online foundation.

Stream low end overwhelms:

- Reduce low-frequency buildup before the limiter or encoder.
- Check tracks, bass, kick, and room mics as a combined system.
- Preserve clarity over huge low end for small speakers.

Stream sounds harsh or fatiguing:

- Check cymbal spill, sibilance, vocal presence, and platform/limiter behavior.
- Use dynamic control where the harshness is intermittent.
- Avoid broad dulling that removes lyric clarity.

## Skill Behavior

When asked for a livestream fix, first classify the workflow: FOH feed, aux/matrix, dedicated broadcast, DAW, or remote. Then recommend moves that fit that staffing model.

Do not tell a one-person volunteer team to run a separate broadcast-style workflow unless the recommendation includes a simplified path.
