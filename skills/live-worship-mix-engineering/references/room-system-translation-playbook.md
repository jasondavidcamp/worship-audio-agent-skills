# Room And System Translation Playbook

Use this when the same mix problem changes depending on where it is heard: headphones, console PFL, board recording, livestream, center room, side seats, balcony, lobby, or overflow.

## Source Material Mined

- Shure, "House of Worship Audio Systems" and "How to Control Feedback in a Sound System": microphone placement, open mics, feedback loops, directional mics, monitor/PA geometry, and gain before feedback.
- Yamaha, "How to Tune a Sound System": reference tracks, walking the room, small system EQ moves, RTA/pink-noise checks, and avoiding over-correction.
- Yamaha, "Equalization": HPF use and practical live EQ framing.

## Core Principle

Separate four layers before prescribing a move:

1. Source layer: performer, instrument, mic choice, mic position, handling, gain.
2. Mix layer: fader balance, EQ, dynamics, effects, buses, matrices.
3. System layer: PA coverage, mains/sub alignment, matrix EQ, gain structure, feedback margin.
4. Room layer: reflections, dead spots, low-frequency buildup, stage wash, congregation absorption.

If the board recording sounds good but the room does not, do not darken or thin the whole mix to solve a system/room problem. If the room feels big but the stream feels small, do not assume the FOH balance is wrong; the stream may need its own low end, ambience, or vocal-to-band balance.

## Triage Questions

- Does the issue exist in PFL/headphones, board recording, stream, and the room, or only in one place?
- Is the problem worse in specific seats, especially sides, front rows, balcony, or under-balcony areas?
- Does the issue change when the room fills with people?
- Are mains, subs, fills, delays, lobby, stream, and recordings fed from the same or different paths?
- Is feedback limiting vocal/speech level before the vocal is actually loud enough?
- Are system EQ, channel EQ, and bus EQ being used for different jobs, or are they fighting each other?
- Has the room been checked with familiar reference tracks at service level?

## Diagnostic Patterns

- Board recording clear, room harsh: suspect PA voicing, reflective room behavior, or too much presence in the system path before cutting every vocal channel.
- Center room good, side seats dull or shouty: suspect coverage, fill delay/level, or speaker aiming before changing the full mix.
- Low end powerful in one area and missing elsewhere: suspect room modes, sub placement/alignment, or seat-to-seat variation before over-boosting bass/kick.
- Vocal feeds back before it feels present: suspect mic-to-source distance, open mics, monitor placement, PA geometry, or excessive gain before adding more compression.
- Stream balanced but room vocal buried: stage volume or PA/system coverage may be masking the vocal in the room.
- Room exciting but stream thin/dead: stream feed likely lacks the acoustic contribution of drums, amps, congregational response, and room energy.

## Lowest-Risk Moves

- Compare the problem through headphones/PFL, board recording, stream, and multiple room positions before touching EQ.
- Walk the room with reference material and the actual worship mix; note seat-specific issues separately from global tonal issues.
- Use small system EQ cuts for room/system problems; avoid large graphic-EQ curves unless a repeatable measurement/listening check confirms the need.
- Improve gain before feedback with mic position, source distance, directional mics, fewer open mics, lower monitor spill, and speaker/mic geometry before heavy EQ.
- Keep channel EQ focused on the source and system EQ focused on the deployment environment.
- When the fix belongs to install/tuning rather than Sunday mixing, state that clearly and give a temporary service-safe workaround.

## Skill Behavior

When this playbook applies, output a diagnosis in this shape:

- Likely layer: source, mix, system, room, or stream path.
- Evidence: where the problem appears and where it does not.
- Immediate Sunday move: small, reversible adjustment.
- Longer-term fix: tuning, routing, stage layout, mic/monitor change, or system review.
