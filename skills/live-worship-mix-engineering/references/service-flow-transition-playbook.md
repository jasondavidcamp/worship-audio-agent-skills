# Service Flow And Transition Playbook

Use this when the audio problem appears between songs, during speech/music handoffs, videos, prayers, response moments, announcements, pastor mic changes, or full-service snapshots.

## Source Material Mined

- ProSoundWeb, "Smooth Operations: Successful Transitions Are The Key To Effective Worship Services": transitions need planned cues, ownership, rehearsal, and audio mute/unmute actions.
- Live Design, "Live Mixing Tips for Worship Services": worship audio must protect speech intelligibility while maintaining musical clarity.
- Shure house-of-worship and feedback guidance: speech systems, open mics, gain before feedback, and mic/loudspeaker relationships shape stable service audio.

## Core Principle

Worship audio is not just the song mix. The service has modes: pre-service, walk-in music, worship, welcome, video, teaching, prayer, response, dismissal, and livestream-only moments. Each mode needs a stable audio state.

## Triage Questions

- What mode is the service in, and what is the primary source: speech, band, playback, prayer, pad, video, or congregation?
- Which mics should be open, muted, reverbed, delayed, or removed from the stream in this mode?
- Are pastor/host/worship leader mics routed consistently to room, stream, lobby, recording, and assistive listening?
- Are music reverbs/delays staying active under speech or prayer?
- Are video and playback levels checked against speech and worship loudness?
- Does the band know whether to stop, vamp, pad, or fade under the handoff?
- Are snapshot, mute group, DCA, and playback cues written in the service plan?

## Diagnostic Patterns

- Speech feels distant after worship: music reverbs, band mics, room mics, or wrong DCA/snapshot state may still be active.
- Pastor mic is late or unstable: transition cue, mute state, gain before feedback, or snapshot recall may be wrong.
- Video playback shocks the room or disappears online: playback level was not checked against the service loudness target.
- Prayer sounds noisy or washy: too many open mics, room mics, FX returns, or band channels remain up.
- Stream loses congregation/room feel during response: room mics or ambience may be muted too aggressively.
- Welcome feels chaotic: no one owns the handoff, and audio/video/lighting/band cues are not aligned.

## Lowest-Risk Moves

- Define repeatable mode states: worship, speech, prayer, video, response, and walk-in/dismissal.
- Build mute groups/DCAs around mode changes: band, vocals, speech, playback, FX returns, room mics, stream ambience.
- Keep speech-safe defaults: fewer open mics, controlled low end, minimal music FX, clear presence, stable gain before feedback.
- Check playback and video audio in advance at service level for both room and stream.
- Decide whether pads/reverbs should carry emotional continuity or be muted for intelligibility.
- Rehearse the 15-20 seconds around key handoffs, especially worship-to-welcome, video-to-teaching, and teaching-to-response.
- Document cue ownership: who calls the transition, who unmutes, who starts playback, who fades the band, and who confirms the pastor/host mic.

## Output Shape

When this playbook applies, answer with:

- Mode state: current and next.
- Audio risks: late mic, wrong mute, feedback, wash, playback mismatch, stream/room mismatch.
- Sunday checklist: the immediate cues and mutes.
- Longer-term system: snapshots, run-sheet notes, scene scope, DCA/mute group layout, and rehearsal point.
