# Worship Mix Rubric

Use this as a scorecard, not as a substitute for listening.

## Default Weights

- Lyric clarity: 25
- Lead vocal emotional placement: 15
- Vocal warmth/smoothness: 10
- Band support and masking control: 15
- Low-end support/control: 10
- Drum energy and groove: 10
- Chorus/section lift: 10
- Fatigue/harshness/noise: 5

For mono church deployment, raise lyric clarity and masking control before width/depth.

When using a live-worship preset-chain approach, also check "source separability": the mix should sound cleaner because bleed, resonances, and dynamics are controlled, not smaller because every source has been over-constrained.

## Dimension Notes

### Lyric Clarity

Ask whether a listener can follow the lead vocal without effort in the densest section.

Strong signals:

- Stable center vocal.
- Consonants clear but not spitty.
- Piano/guitars do not cover the vocal's body or presence.
- BGVs support without confusing the lead.

Weak signals:

- Vocal measures bright but still feels buried.
- Compression makes the vocal constant but emotionally flat.
- Reverb/delay pushes the vocal away from the congregation.

### Lead Vocal Emotional Placement

The vocal should feel like it is leading worship, not pasted onto the band and not swallowed by it.

Score down when:

- Vocal is technically audible but detached.
- Tuning/compression draws attention.
- The vocal does not rise emotionally with the chorus.
- Serial vocal processing makes every phrase equally loud and equally urgent.

### Source Separability

Live worship mixes often need expansion, dynamic EQ, and multiband control because multiple open mics and stage sources blur together.

Score up when:

- Lead vocal has a clear lane without sounding gated.
- BGVs support without smearing consonants.
- Piano, acoustic, and keys have body without covering vocal warmth.
- Drums add motion without cymbal wash hiding vocal presence.

Score down when:

- Expansion chops phrasing.
- Compression raises bleed between phrases.
- Bus color makes the band impressive but masks the lyric.

### Band Support And Masking

The band should make the song feel full while leaving the vocal lane open.

Common masking checks:

- Piano/acoustic body versus vocal warmth: roughly 150-500 Hz.
- Guitars/keys versus vocal intelligibility: roughly 1.5-4 kHz.
- Cymbals/sibilance versus vocal smoothness: roughly 5-10 kHz.

### Low End

For CityAlight-style worship, low end should support rather than dominate.

Score down when:

- Kick/bass blur together.
- Low end disappears in quiet sections.
- Sub energy makes the mix feel modern-pop heavy instead of congregational.

### Chorus Lift

A good worship mix should make larger song sections feel larger without only getting louder.

Check:

- More vocal confidence.
- More band width/density if stereo matters.
- More drum/bass motion.
- Clearer BGV support.
- No harshness spike.

## Grade Shape

- `95-100`: excellent and taste-approved.
- `90-94`: strong candidate; only small preference changes remain.
- `85-89`: usable but one meaningful weakness remains.
- `75-84`: directionally okay, needs another engineering pass.
- `<75`: not aligned with aimpoint or has technical failures.

Never give a high score to a clipped render, a vocal-buried render, or a mix that fails the user's explicit taste call.
