# Rhythm Section And Low-End Playbook

Use this when kick, bass, drums, low keys, tracks, or subs are not providing a clear foundation for the band, or when the low end masks the vocal and harmonic instruments.

## Source Material Mined

- Church Production, "A Firm Foundation: Mixing Kick and Bass Guitar": kick and bass should complement instead of compete, with attention to fundamental, mid-bass, and attack/definition ranges.
- Church Production, "Mixing Techniques": modern worship mixes often build from kick, drums, and bass while still keeping vocals present.
- Live Design, "Live Mixing Tips for Worship Services": speech and lyric clarity remain primary, and kick/bass choices should not fight in the same frequency space.
- Yamaha, "Equalization": HPFs remove unwanted low-frequency energy and improve intelligibility.

## Core Principle

Low end has a job, not just a level. Decide whether the kick provides punch, the bass provides sustain/pitch, the subs provide weight, and the drums provide section energy. If everything owns the same low-frequency lane, the mix gets big but unclear.

## Triage Questions

- What is the song section asking for: intimate support, steady groove, lift, or full-band impact?
- Is the bass functioning as the sustain/pitched extension of the kick, or is it covering the kick?
- Does kick attack read on small speakers and stream, or only in subs?
- Are keys, tracks, acoustic guitar, toms, floor wedge spill, or room mics adding unmanaged low mids?
- Is low end consistent across room seats, or is the issue mostly system/room translation?
- Does compression/limiting clamp the whole mix when kick or bass hits?
- Are kick, bass, and snare supporting the vocal phrase, or distracting from it?

## Diagnostic Patterns

- Kick and bass blur together: too much shared energy in sub or mid-bass ranges, unclear role assignment, or excessive sustain.
- Low end disappears on stream: insufficient harmonic/attack content, missing bass articulation, or stream path high-pass/limiting.
- Low end huge in room but thin in board recording: room/sub contribution is doing more work than the mix.
- Vocal loses warmth/clarity when band enters: bass, low keys, piano left hand, guitars, or tracks may be filling the vocal body range.
- Chorus does not lift: rhythm section may be dynamically static even if the channel tones are good.
- Mix pumps or ducks on big hits: bus/master compression or limiter may be reacting to unmanaged sub energy.

## Lowest-Risk Moves

- Assign roles before EQ: kick attack/weight, bass pitch/sustain, snare backbeat, overheads/cymbals texture, low keys/tracks support.
- High-pass non-foundation sources so vocal, kick, and bass do not fight avoidable rumble.
- EQ kick and bass in context, not only solo; check whether each move improves the combined groove.
- Use low-mid cuts before broad top boosts when the rhythm section makes the mix cloudy.
- Add definition/harmonics to bass or kick for stream/small-speaker translation instead of only adding sub.
- Control sustain with damping, gates, envelope choices, or light dynamics before crushing the bus.
- Watch master/bus gain reduction during kick and bass hits; if the whole mix ducks, fix low-end energy before making the limiter work harder.

## Output Shape

For rhythm-section diagnoses, answer with:

- Foundation role: what kick, bass, snare, and low keys/tracks should each own.
- Current failure: blur, thinness, pumping, masking, weak lift, or room-only size.
- First move: one fader/EQ/dynamics/source move.
- Translation check: room, stream, small speaker, mono, and section contrast.
