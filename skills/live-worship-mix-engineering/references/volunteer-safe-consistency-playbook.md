# Volunteer-Safe Consistency Playbook

Use this when the goal is repeatable Sunday audio with mixed-skill volunteers, limited rehearsal time, or a need for console-neutral starting practices.

Mined from public sources:

- Yamaha, Sound System Tips and Tricks: https://www.yamaha.com/US/houseofworship/downloadables/Audio-System-How-to-Guides/Sound-System-Tips-Tricks-Updated.pdf
- Yamaha, Roadmap to a Consistent Mix: https://www.yamaha.com/US/houseofworship/downloadables/Audio-System-How-to-Guides/Roadmap-to-a-Consistent-Mix.pdf
- Yamaha, Equalization: https://hub.yamaha.com/proaudio/livesound/eq/

## First Principle

Volunteer-safe audio favors repeatable routines, simple defaults, and small reversible moves. A beginner should be able to recover the mix when something goes wrong.

## Default Sunday Routine

Before rehearsal:

- Start from a known-good scene or template.
- Confirm inputs, names, phantom power, wireless battery/RF state, and basic routing.
- Engage HPF/low cut on sources that do not need deep low end, especially speech and vocals.
- Leave enough headroom on channels, buses, matrices, and stream output.
- Confirm emergency fallback paths: spare vocal mic, spare DI, and a plan for wireless or computer failure.

During soundcheck:

- Build the mix from the most important sources outward.
- Use PFL/solo to hear what each live microphone captures besides the intended source.
- Set vocal intelligibility before chasing polish.
- Listen for low-mid mud and harsh presence in context, not only solo.
- Avoid engaging compression or gates until the source level and basic tone make sense.

During service:

- Ride faders and mutes actively.
- Keep speech transitions clean: mute music reverbs, crowd mics, and unused stage mics when they blur the message.
- Make small changes. Do not rebuild the mix mid-service unless there is a technical failure.

After service:

- Save useful notes, not every knob.
- Review a room recording and/or board recording.
- Convert repeated problems into template changes, training notes, or musician/source notes.

## Volunteer-Friendly Starting Moves

Vocal needs clarity:

- Confirm mic technique and gain before adding EQ.
- HPF first.
- Add presence gently only if it helps words, not just brightness.
- If 2-5 kHz becomes harsh, reduce the harsh area instead of pulling all highs down.

Vocal is muddy:

- Check proximity effect, low-mid buildup, and stage spill.
- Try a small cut in the low-mid region instead of broad high boosts.

Band is cloudy:

- Reduce low-mid buildup from piano, acoustic guitar, keys, and guitar sources before cutting the whole mix.
- Make space for the vocal rather than just raising it.

Kick and bass do not work together:

- Decide which source owns low-end weight and which source owns attack.
- Do not boost both in the same low-frequency area without checking the combined result.

Mix lacks consistency between operators:

- Standardize the soundcheck order.
- Standardize starting scenes and subgroup names.
- Use virtual soundcheck or multitrack playback for training when available.
- Review the same reference service recording together.

## Team Review Workflow

Use recordings as the least-confrontational feedback tool.

1. Capture a board mix and, when possible, a room recording.
2. Review the same song section with the team.
3. Name the top one or two problems only.
4. Convert each problem into a next-Sunday action.
5. Update the template only after the issue repeats or the fix is clearly useful.

Avoid turning review into subjective preference chaos. Anchor comments to the worship mix priorities, and use `band-sound-aimpoint` when a preference should become a reusable rule:

- Can the congregation understand the words?
- Does the band support singing?
- Is the sound comfortable and non-fatiguing?
- Did the stream or room translate the service honestly?

## Skill Behavior

When advising a volunteer team, prefer:

- "Try this at soundcheck" over "replace the chain."
- "Make this a template default" only when it is broadly safe.
- "Ask the musician or speaker to change source behavior" when the problem is upstream.
- "Record and review" when disagreement is mostly subjective.

Do not recommend fragile moves that require expert timing unless the user has the people and rehearsal time to execute them.
