# Aimpoint Vocabulary

Use these words to translate taste calls into mix direction.

## Common Positive Targets

- Lead-forward: the lead vocal stays clearly in front without feeling detached from the band.
- Congregational: lyrics are intelligible and the band supports singing rather than dominating it.
- Warm but clear: low mids feel supportive, not cloudy.
- Open top: air and cymbals feel extended without sibilance or fatigue.
- Controlled energy: compression and bus processing keep excitement steady without flattening dynamics.
- Natural live polish: audience/room feel present but not washy.

## Watch Words

- Thin: not enough body, low mids, or vocal chest.
- Boxy: buildup around the lower mids makes the source feel enclosed.
- Harsh: presence/edge overwhelms intelligibility or becomes fatiguing.
- Dull: top end and transient detail are underrepresented.
- Buried: vocal or melody loses priority.
- Hyped: exciting at first but too bright, compressed, or low-end heavy over time.

## Decision Pattern

When the user gives a taste call, record:

- Candidate or render name.
- What section was heard.
- Positive words.
- Negative words.
- Likely metric correlate, if known.
- Next move.
