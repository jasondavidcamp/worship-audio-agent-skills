# Worship Mix Hierarchy Examples

Use this when translating an aimpoint into practical priorities: what should lead, what should support, what should stay out of the way, and how a church team might describe success.

Mined from public sources:

- Cross Point Church Audio Engineer Guide: https://crosspoint.tv/wp-content/uploads/2022/07/AudioEngineerGuide_2022.pdf
- Church Production, Building a Rock-Solid Audio Mix: https://www.churchproduction.com/education/building-a-rock-solid-audio-mix/
- Church Production, Mixing Techniques: https://www.churchproduction.com/education/mixing_techniques/

## First Principle

Aimpoint language should guide attention. The question is not only "is each source audible?" but "does the mix tell the congregation what to listen to?"

## Universal Worship Hierarchy

Use this as the default unless the user's church has a different stated value:

1. Message and lyrics.
2. Lead vocal or worship leader cue.
3. Band foundation that supports singing.
4. BGVs/choir/congregational support.
5. Energy, width, ambience, and polish.

## Style-Dependent Variants

Natural congregational:

- Lead vocal and melody are primary.
- Band is warm and supportive.
- Drums lift sections without dominating.
- BGVs invite singing but stay behind the lead.
- Low end supports; top end stays smooth.

Modern arena:

- Lead vocal remains primary through dense sections.
- Drums/bass/guitars/tracks create lift and scale.
- BGVs and crowd energy help the room feel large.
- Width and ambience matter, but lyric clarity still wins.

Acoustic/folk:

- Lead vocal, acoustic/piano, and lyric tone are central.
- Bass/drums may be lighter or absent.
- Dynamics can breathe.
- Reverb should feel natural, not hidden behind polish.

Ambient/spontaneous:

- Leadership cue and lyric intelligibility remain the guardrails.
- Pads/verbs/room can carry emotion.
- Space must not erase direction.
- Crowd/room response can be part of the target.

Gospel/choir-supported:

- Lead vocal anchors the message.
- Choir/BGVs are a major emotional and congregational layer.
- Rhythmic pocket and harmonic clarity matter more than huge stereo tricks.

## Attention Diagnostics

Ask:

- What source draws the ear first?
- Is that the source that should lead the moment?
- If the mix feels big, is it big because the band supports the lyric or because the band covers the lyric?
- Are BGVs helping the congregation sing or competing with the lead?
- Does reverb create worshipful space or distance the leader?
- Is low end energizing the room/stream or becoming the main attraction?

## Depth And Distance

Use volume, tone, ambience, and panning to create a hierarchy:

- Louder, brighter, drier, and more transient usually feels closer.
- Softer, darker, wetter, or less transient usually feels farther away.
- BGVs can sit behind the lead through level, EQ, and ambience.
- Instruments can be audible without living at the same depth as the vocal.

Do not make every source equally forward unless the style intentionally wants a wall of sound.

## Aimpoint Phrases

Good target phrases:

- "Lead vocal leads without feeling pasted on."
- "Band energy sits directly under the lyric."
- "BGVs make the congregation feel invited."
- "Low end is warm and anchored, not the point of the mix."
- "The bridge opens up emotionally without becoming abrasive."
- "The stream feels like the room, but with clearer vocal hierarchy."

Weak target phrases:

- "Make it professional."
- "Make it big."
- "Make it like the record."
- "Add polish."

Convert weak phrases into hierarchy:

```text
"Make it big" could mean stronger drums, more BGV/crowd energy, wider guitars/tracks, more reverb, or louder chorus automation. Ask which part of the experience should feel bigger before changing the mix.
```

## Skill Behavior

When critiquing an aimpoint or candidate, state the hierarchy before the move:

```text
The target is modern arena worship, but this candidate puts cymbal/keys width ahead of lead vocal clarity. Keep the style, but move the attention hierarchy back toward vocal first, band lift second, width third.
```
