## First Principle

Classify the target before judging it. "More worshipful" is not a mix target. "Natural congregational with warm lead vocal and restrained band density" is.

## Style Families

### Natural Congregational

Common references:

- Scripture-forward congregational songs.
- Hymn-modern hybrids.
- Many melody-led local church arrangements.

Aimpoint:

- Lead vocal and congregational melody are unmistakable.
- Band supports the song without feeling like a performance showcase.
- Low end is warm and supportive rather than modern/sub-heavy.
- Drums and guitars are controlled; acoustic/piano often carry the harmonic language.
- Top end is smooth and low fatigue.
- BGVs support singing more than width spectacle.

Risks:

- Too polite or small.
- Piano/acoustic low-mid cloud.
- Lead vocal too dry or exposed.
- Drums lacking section lift.

### Acoustic / Folk Worship

Common references:

- Acoustic worship sets.
- Acoustic training or discipleship-focused arrangements.
- Small-band hymn or psalm settings.

Aimpoint:

- Acoustic/piano/voice relationship is central.
- Strum transient and vocal diction matter more than huge width.
- Room and ambience should feel human, not washed.
- Dynamics can breathe more than in track-heavy modern worship.

Risks:

- Acoustic guitar becomes clicky or thin.
- Vocal feels naked rather than intimate.
- Low end disappears when there is no bass/drum foundation.

### Modern Arena Worship

Common references:

- Large-room live worship arrangements.
- Big live-album worship with extended builds and crowd energy.

Aimpoint:

- Strong section lift.
- Layered vocals and big bridges.
- Drums, bass, and guitars/tracks provide drive.
- Wider stereo and more ambience are part of the emotional language.
- Lead vocal stays clear through dense choruses.

Risks:

- Formulaic loudness without emotional contour.
- Cymbal/track brightness masking vocals.
- Too much low-end impact for a local room or stream.
- Arrangement density greater than the local team can reproduce.

### Ambient / Spontaneous Worship

Common references:

- Long-form or spontaneous worship.
- Prayer-room and extended ministry moments.

Aimpoint:

- Space, pads, tails, and emotional pacing are central.
- The mix may intentionally leave room for speaking, ad-libs, prayer, and congregation.
- Vocal intimacy and room response matter more than tight radio structure.

Risks:

- Lyrics lose clarity.
- Reverb/pads blur leadership cues.
- Mix feels directionless if the team cannot lead the dynamic arc.
- Stream feels washy without intentional audience/room control.

### Track-Heavy Pop Worship

Common references:

- Modern CCM/pop-worship productions with loops, synths, programmed elements, and stacked vocals.

Aimpoint:

- Tight low end, controlled transients, and polished top.
- Loops/tracks create motion and width.
- Vocal tuning/doubling may be more audible and acceptable.
- Section changes can be production-led.

Risks:

- Local band feels buried under tracks.
- Congregation becomes audience rather than participant.
- Mono-first systems lose width-dependent information.
- Volunteers cannot maintain the balance live.

### Gospel-Influenced / Choir-Supported Worship

Common references:

- Worship with choir, strong BGV answers, call-and-response, or gospel rhythmic language.

Aimpoint:

- Lead vocal is still the anchor, but BGVs/choir are a major emotional engine.
- Rhythmic pocket and bass/drum relationship matter.
- Harmonic density should feel powerful, not smeared.

Risks:

- BGVs overpower the lead lyric.
- Dense mids hide the band foundation.
- Reverb makes choir beautiful but unintelligible.

## Hybrid Targets

Most real churches are hybrids. Name the blend:

- "Natural congregational restraint with a little large-room lift."
- "Acoustic worship clarity with modern livestream polish."
- "Arena worship energy, but mono-safe and volunteer-simple."
- "Ambient prayer-space verses with tight modern choruses."

When creating a hybrid, choose:

- Primary value: congregational clarity, emotional lift, intimacy, or production energy.
- Secondary flavor: width, ambience, drum drive, acoustic warmth, choir support, or track polish.
- Explicit limits: not too glossy, not too ambient, not too band-forward, not too sparse.

## Skill Behavior

When the user gives a vague target, map it to a style family first:

```text
"Natural and worshipful" probably means natural congregational unless the user also asks for huge builds, tracks, or ambient spontaneous moments. I will target warm lead vocal, smooth top, supportive low end, and clear melody before I chase width or big-room polish.
```
